Privacy policy

Last updated 11 November 2026.

Bentotachi is a recipe, dinner-plan, shopping-list and pantry app made by The Koi Pond, a small group of friends. This page explains what happens to your information when you use it.

The short version

Bentotachi doesn't send your information to the developer or to anyone else. There are no ads, no analytics and no tracking. Everything you add stays on your phone, or, if you choose to connect to a Bentotachi server to share with your household, on that server, which you or someone you know runs.

What Bentotachi stores, and where

When you use Bentotachi, it stores what you put into it: your account (a username, password, display name and optionally an email address, profile picture and colour), recipes and their photos, ratings, notes (including the ones on the fridge door), the dinner plan, the shopping list, and the pantry with its items' photos and packets.

On this phone only (the usual way to start): Bentotachi runs its own small server inside the app, reachable only from your phone, and keeps everything in the app's private storage. Nothing leaves the phone unless you save a backup, which goes wherever you choose (like Google Drive). Bentotachi turns off Android's automatic backup to Google, so Bentotachi's own backups are the only copies.

With a Bentotachi server (for sharing with a household): everything is stored on the server you log in to. That server is run by you or by the person who invited you (for example on a computer or Raspberry Pi in their home), not by the developer. The people with an account on the same server can see the household's recipes, lists and plans, as sharing them is what Bentotachi is for. The person running the server can see everything stored on it. The app also keeps a copy of the household's recipes, shopping list, meal plan, pantry and fridge door notes on your phone, so you can use them without a connection, and remembers your settings and login there. Logging out removes that copy.

What Bentotachi connects to

  • Your Bentotachi server, if you use one, to load and save everything above.
  • Recipe websites you choose. When you save a recipe from a link, Bentotachi (on your phone, or your server) fetches that page to read the recipe from it, and the photo from it (and its steps' photos, if you turned on Bring step photos). When you tap a site's Categories on Browse, it fetches that site's home page, and the category pages you open, to list them. When you open a site, a store's page or a search from Bentotachi, it opens in a browser and that website's own privacy policy applies.
  • Open Food Facts, when you scan a barcode Bentotachi doesn't know yet, and to find a photo for a pantry item that has a barcode. Bentotachi (on your phone, or your server) sends only the barcode number to Open Food Facts, a free, open database of food products, to find the product's name, brand, size and photo. Nothing else about you is sent.
  • Store pages you link to a pantry item. To find a photo for an item without one, Bentotachi (on your phone, or your server) fetches the store's product page you linked and takes the product photo from it.

Bentotachi doesn't contact the developer, and doesn't use any advertising or analytics services.

Permissions

Bentotachi uses your internet connection to reach recipe websites and, if you use one, your Bentotachi server. When you add a photo to a recipe, a pantry item or your profile, or a saved web page to import, you pick the file yourself; Bentotachi only gets the file you choose. When you tap Take a photo, your phone's own camera app takes it and hands Bentotachi just that photo: Bentotachi doesn't ask for camera access.

When you start your first step timer, Bentotachi asks to show notifications, and to set alarms and reminders, so the timer can ring when it's done even with your phone locked. Your phone shows that notification itself; nothing is sent anywhere.

Scanning a barcode uses Google's own scanner on your phone (part of Google Play services), which handles the camera itself. Bentotachi doesn't ask for camera access and only receives the barcode number. Google Play services may send Google basic information about how its scanner is working, under Google's privacy policy.

Deleting your information

You can delete recipes, notes, lists and your photos in the app at any time. To delete your account, open Profile in the app and tap Delete my account: your account, picture and ratings are deleted from the server, while recipes and lists you added stay for your household. On a Bentotachi that's on your phone only, the same place says Delete everything, which deletes all of Bentotachi's data from the phone. Uninstalling the app also removes everything it kept on your phone.

Without the app: see Deleting your Bentotachi account and data, which explains how, whether it's on your phone or a household server.

Children

Bentotachi is a household app for adults. It isn't made for children and doesn't knowingly collect their information.

Changes
